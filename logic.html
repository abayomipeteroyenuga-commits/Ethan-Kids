MATHS & LOGIC

Think like a problem solver

Learn to notice patterns, compare possibilities and explain how you know an answer is correct.

Solve it step by step

1 · Spot a pattern

A pattern is a rule that repeats or changes in a predictable way. Look at what changes between steps.

Worked example: 2, 4, 6, 8 → add 2 each time.
Try it: Predict the next three numbers in 3, 6, 12, 24. Explain your rule.

2 · Break it into parts

Decomposition means dividing a large problem into smaller, manageable steps.

Worked example: To plan a party: choose a place → make a list → plan activities → check supplies.
Try it: Break “design a game” into at least four small jobs.

3 · Compare and classify

Classify objects by a property, such as color, shape or size. Some objects fit more than one group.

Worked example: A red triangle is in both the red group and the triangle group.
Try it: Make two groups for objects in your room. Which objects fit both?

4 · Find the missing information

Before solving a problem, identify what you know and what you still need.

Worked example: If three boxes hold 4 pencils each, the total is 3 × 4 = 12.
Try it: A game score doubles each round. What else must you know to calculate the score after four rounds?

5 · Test your reasoning

A good solution should work for more than one example. Look for a counterexample that breaks your rule.

Worked example: “All numbers ending in 2 are even” can be tested with 12, 22 and 32.
Try it: Invent a rule about numbers. Test it with five examples and one tricky case.

6 · Explain your thinking

The answer matters, but so does the reason. Use words, drawings or a table to show your steps.

Worked example: “The next number is 16 because each step adds 4.”
Try it: Explain to a friend why 1, 4, 9, 16 continues with 25.

Interactive number-pattern challenge

What comes next: 5, 10, 20, 40, … ?

Stretch: Make a pattern of your own and ask someone to explain its rule.
